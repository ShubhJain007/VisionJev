Qwen3-VL-4B, round 1:
broad training, a reasoning delta, and weight averaging

Results of the one-night 4B run (Phase A on all 117 datasets, Phase B on a new hard-reasoning set), the two A/B weight averages, and the same evaluation suite as the 30B and 2B: JevBench public, zero-shot, never-seen tasks, held-out wording, robustness and latency. With a diagnosis of what worked, what did not, and the round-2 plan.

Verdict: ship 4B soup 75% B. The four 4B checkpoints are tied outside JevBench (zero-shot, never-seen, held-out wording and MMLU agree within 0.6 points), and on JevBench every one of 4B Phase B, 4B soup 50/50, 4B soup 75% B is within one item of the best; among those, 4B soup 75% B has the lowest calibration error (0.046), balances the original (93.1%) and hard (55.0%) tiers, and has the fewest option-order flips. Across the shared suite (section 4) the 4B matches the 30B on zero-shot accuracy and held-out wording, is better calibrated, and answers an image decision about six times faster; the 30B keeps its lead on knowledge (MMLU) and on the hard tier. The remaining gap to Imajev is almost entirely the hard tier.

6. Findings

  1. The 4B is the right product tier. On everything except knowledge and the hard tier it matches the 30B, with better calibration and ~6× lower latency; on the estimated composite it beats the 30B, whose cost axis is penalised.
  2. The format fix worked. The original tier went from 63.9% (2B) to 95.8% (Phase A); describe_labels plus the 4,096-token middle-cut budget is the likely cause (not separately ablated).
  3. Phase B buys reasoning and costs format. Hard +7.3 points (multi_hop, ambiguous, long_policy up), original −4.1 (3 of 72 items): the forgetting Imajev reported. Weight averaging trades the two smoothly: 50/50 restores the original tier (95.8%) and keeps half the hard gain (53.2%); 75% B sits between (93.1% / 55.0%) with the best calibration of any 4B (ECE 0.046). All four checkpoints are equal on JevBench within one item.
  4. Dates and time zones did not move (temporal_numeric 7–13%): ~500 Phase B steps over 3 generator kinds are not enough for multi-step calendar arithmetic in one pass.
  5. Option-order flips are unchanged (~3%): an architecture problem (per-option read-out on a causal backbone), not data.
  6. Coverage was thin: Phase A saw ~20% of one pass; long-tail datasets got ~2k draws each.
